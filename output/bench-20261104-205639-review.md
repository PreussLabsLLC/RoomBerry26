Verified command bytes for 20/30 and 45/54 and their swapped pairs: the serial
wire contains the intended commands, with correct right-then-left OI ordering.
The received requested-velocity fields also preserve the intended ordering,
although quantized. This is not an obvious wheel-argument swap in the runner.
Small steering differences can be swallowed by the low-speed response: the
20/30 pair has mean imbalance opposite the intended direction; 45/54 almost
loses the intended differential. Short windows/noise limit conclusions about
an exact turn direction from a single near-equal pair. Larger 30/50 differences
have a clear response in this unloaded experiment.

## Equal speed does not produce stable equality

Paired forward sweep: 40/40 yielded 58.8/68.7; 50/50 yielded 60.1/67.3.
For equal 30/30, initial sweep yielded 37.6/39.8; repeat 1 yielded 44.7/37.8;
repeat 2 yielded 44.8/38.4 mm/s. Its left-minus-right imbalance changed from
-2.2 to +6.9/+6.4 mm/s. Therefore a fixed per-wheel trim inferred from one
stage/run would be unreliable. Reverse, solo and opposing-wheel patterns also
differ. This agrees with the previous partial run's broad low-speed plateau,
but its +30/+30 pair was 42.81/40.56 and +50/+50 was 65.89/68.00, confirming
that exact low-speed imbalance does not reproduce perfectly between runs.

## Current and stopping

No sampled overcurrent flag. Whole-run sampled motor-current peaks were
0.739 A left (sweep500/left+) and 0.795 A right (sweep400/right+). Active steady
hold means, across this schedule, were 0.087/0.099 A. The right motor averaged
more current under this profile, but different unloaded motor losses and sensor
calibration prevent interpreting that alone as a mechanical fault. Peak values
are sampled at ~19 Hz and may miss shorter transients.

Median detected startup delay was ~0.122 s for both wheels. Median detected
stop delay ~0.126 s, with maxima ~0.386 s. At 30 mm/s commands, typical stop
latency was ~0.12–0.13 s and sampled coast ~3.8 nominal mm per wheel; at 500,
median stop latency ~0.33 s and coast ~83–85 nominal mm. Coast includes the
first post-zero sample interval and can overestimate pure after-command travel.
The stop threshold is 10 mm/s sustained for 0.5 s, not an exact zero-speed edge.
These measurements reinforce why a wrong-direction guard evaluated 19 ms after
a pivot reversal can fire prematurely. A 200 ms mission loop plus ~120 ms stop
latency can permit several degrees of additional unloaded wheel-derived rotation.
That is a timing illustration, not a prediction of loaded chassis yaw.

## Implications for the grout run

1. This provides a concrete mechanism for weak or wrong-way small steering at
   20..50 mm/s commands: wheel response has plateaus and unstable left/right
   imbalance. It is strong evidence of a control limitation, not proof of the
   full floor-veering cause. A bench stand cannot measure tire traction or slip.
2. Retain measured braking/settling before reversing pivots; anchor the
   wrong-direction guard after command establishment and bounded settling.
3. Do not apply one global speed multiplier or permanent left/right trim from
   this unloaded run. First verify wheel/tick scaling and compare a short loaded
   low-speed test; encoder feedback can then regulate wheel speed differences.
4. Keep tag localization/scan-consensus fixes as separate work: motor compliance
   cannot explain or correct noisy stationary AprilTag poses.
