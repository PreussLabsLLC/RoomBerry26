# Three-gap wall-sensor trial

The right signal clearly distinguishes at-wall from the two other positions.
At10mm its median has fallen about92% from the at-wall reading, and its1–10
range overlaps the0–2 range at50mm. At50mm it is effectively at the observed
background level. No meaningful50mm distance-regulating signal is demonstrated;
this trial provides no100mm measurement. The extra numeric resolution did not
provide a useful wider sensing range on this surface and geometry.

The right median is roughly four times the old median near the wall (61 vs15).
That larger number should not be mistaken for four times the sensing distance.
Neither raw intensity series is a calibrated distance measurement. These three
points do not justify fitting a reliable millimetres-per-count conversion.

Black-baseboard reflectivity is a plausible explanation, but this trial has no
white-surface comparison and cannot isolate reflectivity from alignment or
sensor geometry. Recorded encoder angle changes during repositioning also mean
this was not an independently verified constant-angle calibration.

Requested and applied wheel speeds remained zero throughout. No front physical
bumper, binary wall, or hard-hazard assertions occurred. WALL stayed in clearance
with a null target: this is a stationary/manual-repositioning sensing trial,
not evidence that autonomous wall following succeeded or failed. Side wall
contact reported by the operator need not assert the front bumpers.

Practical implication: neither available signal demonstrates the desired
50–100mm stand-off on this baseboard. Lowering the threshold cannot restore
missing distance information, and close wall tracking would not establish
clearance for the higher DIN plug/cabinet overhang. A known-distance white-board
comparison would test the reflectivity hypothesis. Maintaining the requested
larger gap needs a usable independent geometry/range reference if these IR
readings remain at background.

Held-window statistics are saved in183024-held-positions.csv. No control
changes, deployment, or robot commands were made during this analysis.
